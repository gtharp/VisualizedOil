Montana: the Board.

The Montana Board of Oil and Gas Conservation decides who shares in a well, and it can pool you whether or not you signed a lease. Royalty owners pay a 14.8% production tax from the first barrel, and many checks also have state income tax withheld. If you learned minerals in Texas, a surprising amount of what you know runs the other way here.

Start here

Top things every Montana mineral owner should know

Tax

Royalty owners pay 14.8% production tax from the first barrel.

The operator pays it and takes your share out of your check, unless your lease says otherwise. Working interest owners get a 0.5% rate for the first 18 months of a new horizontal well. Royalty owners do not. 15-36-304, -310

Tax

Many royalty checks also have Montana income tax withheld.

Larger payors withhold at the state's top income tax rate (5.65% for 2026). The money is not lost: it is a credit you claim on a Montana return. Qualifying tax-exempt organizations can stop it, but only with a written request that reaches the payor and the Department of Revenue before November 1 of the prior year. 15-30-2538, -2539

The Board, not your lease, decides who shares in a well.

Montana builds drilling units by Board order after a public hearing. Your share of a well is your acres in the unit divided by the unit's total acres, times your royalty, as fixed by the order for that well. 82-11-201, -202

Unleased? You can be force-pooled.

If you refuse to join a well after written demand, the operator recovers 200% of your share of drilling and completion costs (plus 100% of surface equipment and operating costs) out of your share of production. Meanwhile you receive a 1/8 royalty. After payout, you own your full working interest share and pay your share of costs. 82-11-202(2)

Answer the 30-day election letter.

Silence counts as a refusal. Penalties also apply after any written demand, and a refusal follows the minerals to the next owner (Phoenix Capital, 2024). 82-11-202(3)

Late royalties earn at least 15% interest.

Accepting a catch-up payment does not by itself give up the interest claim, so reserve it in writing. 82-10-103; 31-1-107

Keep your address current in the county records.

Board hearing notices and well proposals go to your address of record at the county clerk and recorder. If an operator cannot find you, after 6 months your money goes into a court-supervised trust. After 5 years without contact, it is presumed abandoned to the Montana Department of Revenue. 82-1-302, -305; 70-9-803

Old minerals do not expire for non-use.

Montana has no dormant mineral act. North Dakota, next door, has one.

Montana reads mineral reservations in favor of the seller.

That is the reverse of the Texas rule. Old deeds in Montana chains of title are construed under Montana law. 70-1-516

Low prices do not excuse a shut-in.

Under Montana's temporary cessation rule, only a sudden stoppage or mechanical breakdown counts, and only if the operator fixes it promptly. (Somont, 2002 and 2006)

Taxes, visualized

Two taxes come off a Montana royalty check

The first is the oil and gas production tax, a tax on the product, paid by the operator and charged to you. The second is state income tax withholding, a prepayment of your own income tax that you settle on a Montana return. They are separate lines on the stub and work differently.

Illustrative only. Post-production deductions, if your lease allows them, are left out. The withholding base is the net Montana royalty reported in Box 2 of your Form 1099-MISC; this illustration assumes that equals the amount left after production tax, and your payor's base may differ. The 14.8% already includes the 0.3% Board privilege and license tax and resource distribution account tax, so nothing is added on top of it 15-36-304(7).

Working interest gets a break. Royalty does not.

The production tax has a reduced rate for new wells, but only for the working interest. Every nonworking interest (royalty, overriding royalty) pays 14.8% in every category, from day one. MCA 15-36-304; MCA 2025, last amended 2023

The production tax

  • Who pays. The operator pays the tax for all owners. Unless a contract or lease provides otherwise, it deducts each royalty owner's share from settlements 15-36-310. The tax is paid quarterly, within 60 days after quarter end 15-36-311.
  • Exempt. Interests owned by the state or local governments 15-36-304(8). Royalties received by tribes under Indian Mineral Leasing Act leases, by the United States as trustee for individual Indians, and by federal, state, county and municipal governments 15-36-309.
  • New-well window. The working interest's 18-month reduced rate starts at first production and runs straight through, whether or not production is continuous. It does not restart for a new owner who buys a well after the window has run (Bluebird Energy LLC v. Dep't of Revenue, 2024 MT 10).
  • The 0.3% is inside the rate. The statute adjusts every rate to include the Board's privilege and license tax and the natural resource distribution account tax, which together total 0.3% 15-36-304(7). From April 1, 2026 the Board set its privilege and license tax at 60% of the rate authorized by statute ARM 36.22.1242(2); 82-11-131. That only changes how the 0.3% is divided. The total rate you pay stays 14.8%.
  • It is not an income tax. Tax-exempt owners still bear it.

Income tax withholding

  • Rate. The payor (the "remitter") withholds the highest marginal individual rate from each royalty payment: 5.65% for 2026, 5.4% from January 1, 2027 15-30-2538, amended 2025; 15-30-2103. The base is the net Montana royalty in Box 2 of Form 1099-MISC.
  • Ignore the old 6%. Before 2025 the statute fixed the rate at 6%. The 2025 amendment tied it to the top income tax rate instead. The Department of Revenue's own withholding web page and many secondary sources still say 6% as of November 2026, and so does the text of the Department's rules (ARM 42.17.601 and 42.17.603, last amended 2024). The statute controls.
  • What it covers. Royalty, overriding royalty, production payments, and any other nonworking interest, including advance royalty payments tied to production. An owner who takes production in kind must send the withholding to the Department itself. ARM 42.17.601
  • Who is withheld on. Every royalty owner, resident or not, unless excluded below. The statute applies to "each royalty payment made to a royalty owner," and neither it nor the Department's withholding page limits it to nonresidents. What matters is that the property is in Montana. 15-30-2537, -2538
  • Who is excluded 15-30-2539: governments; tribes, for on-reservation Indian Mineral Leasing Act production; the United States as trustee for individual Indians; publicly traded partnerships; organizations exempt under 15-31-102; a royalty owner that is the remitter itself; and small producers averaging under 100,000 barrels of oil and 500 MMcf of gas a year over three prior calendar years. The Department tells each oil and gas producer by September 15 whether it must withhold the next year. The small-producer exception does not cover a payor that is not itself a producer, and if a producer's oil or its gas crosses the line, both are withheld on. ARM 42.17.601(2), 42.17.603(1), (3), (5)
  • Small payments. The Department lets payors skip withholding on royalty owners paid less than $2,000 a year or less than $166 a month 15-30-2539(3); ARM 42.17.603(12).

Tax-exempt owners: the November 1 letter

A qualifying exempt organization is not excluded automatically. It must send a report to both the payor and the Department of Revenue, and the report can be a letter 15-30-2539(2)(d); ARM 42.17.603(10):

  • on the organization's letterhead, requesting exemption from MCA 15-30-2536 through 15-30-2547;
  • received by the payor and the Department before November 1 of the year before the exemption year (a letter that arrives November 1 or later counts toward the year after);
  • the Department notifies the organization and the payor of acceptance or denial within 30 days;
  • no annual renewal unless the Department asks.

Publicly traded partnerships use the same letter, which must also state that the partnership is publicly traded ARM 42.17.603(9). When a producer sells, the buyer becomes the remitter and keeps withholding ARM 42.17.603(4). The new payor has not received the old letter, so send it again after any acquisition or change in revenue system.

Getting money back. The withholding is claimed as a credit on a Montana individual or corporate income tax return, with the Form 1099-MISC attached ARM 42.17.602(1)(a). Owners of a pass-through entity claim their share from the Montana Schedule K-1. An estate or trust and its beneficiaries share the credit in proportion to the royalty income each reports ARM 42.17.602(1)(b)-(g). An exempt owner withheld on in error claims a refund from the Department; the payor cannot refund tax it already remitted. The refund deadline is the later of the general assessment period or 1 year from payment 15-30-2609(2)(a).

Read your Form 1099-MISC. Every payor must furnish one (or a Form 1042-S for a foreign person) by January 31, even a payor exempt from withholding ARM 42.17.605(1)(c)-(e). Box 16 (state tax withheld) and Box 17 (payer's state number) show Montana withholding by payor.

Forced pooling

How the Board puts you in a well

In Montana the Board, not the lease, builds the unit. It creates a temporary spacing unit by order for a new well. After the well, it sets a permanent spacing unit and, if needed, pools all interests in it. Current Bakken orders use two- to eight-section units, 200-foot heel and toe and 500-foot lateral setbacks, and allow several additional wells per unit. MCA 82-11-201, -202

From unit to payout

  4. Board hearingNotice mailed to your county address of record, plus published notice.20+ days notice · 82-11-141(4)
  5. Pooling orderTerms must be just and reasonable. Pooled production counts as produced from each tract.Within 30 days · 82-11-141(6)

The steps are not always in calendar order. Pooling after the wells are completed is normal practice, so a pooling order can come years after the election that decides an owner's economics (Phoenix Capital, 2024). Since 2023 the operator may spud before the 30 days run, but the election window still runs its full 30 days.

The penalties for a refusing owner

Recovered only out of that owner's share of production, and mandatory once a refusal follows written demand. 82-11-202(2)

200%of your share of drilling, completion, and in-well equipment costs
100%of your share of new surface equipment and of operating costs until payout
1/8landowner royalty for an unleased owner (less burdens on its interest) until payout

On request, the operator must send an unleased owner monthly statements of costs, production, and proceeds 82-11-202(2)(d).

What the operator must show

A pooling order requires 82-11-202(1)(b):

  • a permanent spacing unit;
  • an applicant that owns an interest or has drilled or proposes a well;
  • an unsuccessful, good-faith attempt to pool voluntarily;
  • a hearing.

The 30-day election

An owner is presumed to refuse if it neither pays nor agrees in writing within 30 days of a notice delivered by written acknowledgment or certified mail. The notice must state the well location, depth, target formations, estimated costs, and spud date. 82-11-202(3), amended 2023

Case to know

Phoenix Capital Group Holdings v. Board of Oil & Gas Conservation, 2024 MT 89 (unanimous)

  • Good faith is judged against the owner at the time of the offers. The operator owes no fresh offer to someone who buys later.
  • Pooling after the wells are completed is normal practice. A pooling order can come years after the election that decides an owner's economics.
  • An election letter is a "written demand" if it carries costs, a deadline, and a penalty warning. Penalties then apply even without the 30-day presumption.
  • No late buy-in. A later offer to pay, by the owner or a buyer, does not undo a refusal.
  • A buyer is bound by the seller's refusal. In this Richland County unit, the buyer received the 12.5% royalty, with penalties charged against its working interest share.

Before buying or inheriting Montana minerals, pull the Board's pooling orders for each unit and ask for every well proposal and election the prior owner received. If a pooling application names you, raise objections at the Board hearing: objections not raised there are treated as waived, and court review is deferential.

MCA 82-10-103(2). Late payments carry interest at the greater of 15% or prime plus 6 points, from the due date 82-10-103(2); 31-1-107.

Interest, and how not to waive it

  • Rate. The greater of 15% or prime plus 6 points, from the due date.
  • When it does not apply. To owners taking production in kind, or while a genuine title or entitlement dispute affects distribution 82-10-103(4). Fast curative work protects the interest claim.
  • Catch-up payments. Accepting sporadic catch-up payments did not waive interest (Textana v. Klabzuba, 2009 MT 401). But Montana law still treats accepting "the whole principal, as such" as a waiver 27-1-214, so reserve interest in writing when suspended funds are released.

Demand and suit

  • Written demand for an accounting; if it is not met in 60 days, suit in the county where the land lies 82-10-101.
  • Payment suits go in the county where the well is located 82-10-103(3).
  • The prevailing party recovers attorney fees either way, so a weak claim can cost you.
  • Limitations 27-2-202: 6 years on a written contract, 5 years unwritten, 3 years for other obligations not founded on a writing. Which period applies depends on how the claim is characterized.

The check stub

Montana requires eleven items on every stub, including the production period, lease identifier, product, volumes, taxes withheld by amount and type, net values, and contact information. Every charge must be listed by line item. A knowing violation is a misdemeanor. This is your tool for testing deductions. 82-10-104

Deductions

No Montana Supreme Court decision settles post-production costs on private (fee) leases, so lease language controls. State trust land leases reserve royalty free of costs and deductions.

Division orders

A Montana division order 82-10-110 must warrant the division of interest, list each payee's name, address and tax ID, and require notice of ownership changes. It cannot alter the lease or relieve the lessee of lease duties. Signing to get paid does not give up lease rights.

Unlocatable owners and unclaimed money

  • A holder may keep an unlocatable owner's bonus, rental, or royalty no more than 6 months. It must then petition the district court to create a trust, with the clerk of court (or the Department of Revenue) as trustee 82-1-302, -305.
  • An owner can file its address and interest with the county clerk and recorder for $5, which creates a presumption of ownership 82-1-306.
  • Mineral proceeds are presumed abandoned to the Department of Revenue after 5 years of dormancy 70-9-803(1)(q).
  • Heirs: search both the county court records and the Department of Revenue unclaimed property database.

Leases, title, and surface

Where Montana title work differs

Recording: race-notice

An unrecorded deed is void against a later good-faith buyer for value who records first 70-21-304. Record promptly in the Montana county: instruments recorded only in another state are not notice there.

Reservations are read for the grantor

In Wicklund v. Sundheim, 2016 MT 62, 383 Mont. 1, 367 P.3d 403 (Richland County):

  • an ambiguous 1953 royalty reservation was construed in favor of the grantor under 70-1-516, the reverse of the Texas rule;
  • an expert's opinion on what the deed language meant was inadmissible;
  • old division orders, a communitization agreement, and years of royalty paid without objection were strong evidence of intent;
  • laches (losing a claim by unreasonable delay) failed, because the owners asserted the interest whenever development occurred.

Keep old division orders and payment records. They are evidence of what a reservation means.

Other title points

  • Eastern Montana counties sold tax-deed land reserving a 6¼% royalty, and some state "mortgage lands" carry a 6¼% state royalty.
  • Homestead-era federal mineral reservations are common.
  • On the Fort Peck Reservation (Valley, Roosevelt, Daniels and Sheridan counties), Indian trust minerals sit in a checkerboard with private land, and federal approvals can slow unit-wide payment.

No dormant mineral act

Montana considered and rejected dormant or abandoned mineral bills in 1977, 1979, 1981, 1983, 1985, 1987, 1989, 1993, 2005, and 2007. No such provision is in the 2025 Code. Severed minerals do not lapse for non-use.

Keeping a lease alive (Somont)

A Montana oil and gas lease ends automatically when paying production stops. The lessee must pass a two-step test, well by well (Somont, 2002 MT 141 and 2006 MT 90):

  • Step 1, paying quantities. Economics count here.
  • Step 2, temporary cessation. The stoppage must come from a sudden stoppage or mechanical breakdown "or the like," with immediate, diligent repair. Low prices, the economy, and the operator's finances do not count.

Bakken leases are mostly held by unit production under pooling orders, so this bites hardest on older vertical-well leases and price-driven shut-ins.

Releasing an expired lease

  • The lessee must record a release within 60 days of expiration. It is a misdemeanor if not cured within 30 days of the owner's written notice 82-1-201.
  • Lawsuit route: written demand at least 20 days before suit. The owner recovers $100, costs, attorney fees, and proven damages; the lessee gets fees if forfeiture is not proved. A lessee that releases after suit is filed still counts toward the owner prevailing (Somont II, 2006 MT 90) 82-1-203.
  • No-lawsuit route: if the lease expired by its terms more than 3 years ago, the owner serves a notice. Unless the lessee records an affidavit that the lease is still in effect within 60 days, the owner records proof of service and the lease is terminated of record 82-1-202.

Pore space

If deeds do not say who owns underground storage space, the surface owner is presumed to own it 82-11-180(3). The rule does not apply on an Indian reservation without tribal action. Mineral-only owners should not assume they own pore space.

Surface damages

  • The operator must give written notice 20 to 180 days before surface disturbance, to the surface owner's county record address 82-10-503.
  • Damages cover lost agricultural production and income, lost land value, and lost improvement value 82-10-504.
  • The parties must negotiate in good faith. The owner may elect annual payments (exploration damage is a lump sum). An annual payment unpaid 60 days after notice is owed double.
  • Defective notice does not stop the mineral owner's right of entry (Pinnacle Gas, 2009 MT 12). Surface use agreements are where real terms are won.

Glossary

Montana terms that differ from Texas

Temporary spacing unit (TSU)A unit the Board creates for a new well in an unproven area.
Permanent spacing unitThe unit set after the well, based on evidence. Pooling orders attach here.
Overlapping TSUA unit laid over older units for one well, limited to that well's production. Montana's answer to the Texas allocation well.
Density orderA Board order allowing additional wells in a unit.
Nonworking interestRoyalty, overriding royalty, and similar cost-free interests. Taxed at 14.8%.
RemitterWhoever pays the royalty and withholds Montana income tax. Sometimes a subsidiary with a different name than the operator. When a producer sells, the buyer becomes the remitter.
"Lease" in Board dataA Department of Revenue reporting unit, not the oil and gas lease.
WildcatThe field label on most Bakken horizontals in Board records.
PayoutWhen the consenting owners have recovered costs plus penalties from a refusing owner's share.

The thing to remember

In Montana the Board holds the unit, and the order for each well fixes your share of it. Your ownership still lives with the county clerk and recorder, and so does the address the Board will use to notify you. Keep it current.
